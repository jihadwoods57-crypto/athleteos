The meal thread

Every plate starts a conversation.

Log lunch and a thread opens on it. The AI Nutritionist's read lands first — foods, macros, a confidence on every line — then your coach, your trainer, you. Not a feed. Not a group text. A conversation attached to evidence.

Your side. Ask it anything.

The AI's read is the first message, not a verdict — reply to it like you'd reply to a person. Ask "is this enough before practice?" and it answers from your targets, your day, and your coach's standard, not the internet.

Correct it in plain words — "that was a double portion" — and the analysis updates on the record: your correction quoted, the new numbers marked Updated analysis. Nothing is silently rewritten.

Your coach's side. Same thread.

A comment on tonight's dinner lands on the athlete's log, next to the plate it's about — not in a message app where it scrolls away. The athlete's thread reads "Coach hasn't reviewed this meal yet" until you weigh in, then "Coach replied" — so silence is never ambiguous, and a reaction counts as a reply.

Everyone in the thread is named — tap the faces and see exactly who's in the room and what they can see. A parent connected as guardian appears there like anyone else: visible to every participant. Transparency, never surveillance.

  • It reads honestly. Per‑food confidence on every plate; a "?" instead of a guess. It can flag possible allergens from what it detects and what you've declared, but it can never confirm a plate is allergen‑free. Don't rely on it for that.
  • It answers from your plan. Your targets, your day, your history — not generic internet advice. It coaches fueling and performance; it never moralizes food.
  • It never decides. It doesn't set targets, doesn't touch the score, and never invents a number a person acts on. Fixed math scores the day. Your professional sets the bar.

One day, start to finish.

This is the whole product. Four steps, every one a real screen from the app, showing what you do and what the app does back. If you can describe this to a teammate, you understand OnStandard.

1

You open the app and point the camera at your plate.

It shows one thing to do next — never a to‑do list to triage. Logging is just the photo: no typing, no food database, no remembering at 11pm what lunch was. It's attached to the day permanently.

2

An AI nutritionist reads it instantly.

It stamps the meal on time, scores the plate's quality, and estimates the macros — naming each food with high, medium or low confidence, and asking one to three questions instead of guessing when something's off the edge of the plate. Confirm it or correct it — it's your plate — and the score on your home screen moves immediately.

3

You check in and close the day honestly.

Before bed: energy, recovery, sleep, confidence — about twenty seconds, and it only counts toward your score if you actually do it. Then: yes, partially, or an off day — captured and shown to your coach, so it's never wasted, even though it isn't part of the math. The app would rather have a real zero than a flattering guess.

4

The day locks at midnight — and shows its math.

Land 80 or better and your streak grows; a day you never opened the app counts as a miss. Every score opens into its parts — what each one contributed, what was left on the table. Nothing about the number is hidden.

And on the other side

Your coach opens one screen and knows.

Every athlete, sorted by score, flagged by what actually happened: on standard, below standard, overdue, or no activity. The athlete who hasn't logged shows a dash — never an invented number. One tap sends a nudge, once per athlete per day.

That's the whole loop. You log; the day scores itself; the person who set the standard sees it. Everything else on this page is detail.

What this is not

Accountability without calorie‑counting.

Most nutrition apps hand a teenager a calorie budget and a food database and call the arithmetic a habit. We won't. On OnStandard the athlete never does the counting: the photo is the log, the AI does the reading, and the day is won by execution — real plates, on time, enough fuel for the work. Show up and fuel. That's the whole job.

  • The counting is the app's job. The daily habit is one photo per meal — no tallying the day at 11pm, no living in a food diary. The AI reads the plate, says how sure it is, and labels every estimate as an estimate. No camera in reach? A quick search or a typed label still logs the meal — and the math is still never yours to do.
  • The score is about showing up. On time counts full, late counts half, skipped counts nothing — and an empty day can't buy a good number. The standard is executing the plan your professional set, not winning an argument with arithmetic.
  • The numbers can be turned off where it matters. An athlete who shouldn't be looking at calories goes on the Intuitive style: the meal read carries no calorie or macro numbers, there are no macro targets to hit, and the score is measured on fueling enough — never on restriction.
  • Weight is a trend, not a scoreboard. It reads as a trend line for the athlete and the people coaching them — never for teammates, never a leaderboard. A parent's dashboard doesn't carry it at all.

Asked honestly, answered honestly

Everything a coach, athlete, or parent asks before they trust the number. No hedging.

How much time does this actually take each day?

One photo per meal — the camera opens straight to the plate, and there's no typing or database searching. Occasionally the AI asks one to three quick questions about something it can't see. Then a check‑in before bed, which the app itself describes as twenty seconds, and one tap to close the day. There's no weekly form to fill out; the Sunday check‑in is the same short scale.

What happens if I miss a day?

It reads as a missed day, honestly — and one is survivable. Your streak forgives one sub‑80 day per rolling seven; a second inside that window ends the run. The forgiven day doesn't itself count toward the streak, and the forgiveness recharges once it's more than a week behind you. A day you simply never opened the app counts as a miss too: going quiet is not a loophole. What the app won't do is pretend the day didn't happen.

Is it really free for athletes and clients?

Yes, on a paying professional's roster: the logging, the AI reads, the daily score, the streak, the breakdown, the coach threads and your full history. They carry the cost because they get the roster‑wide value. There is also an optional personal membership that adds written coaching — a monthly report and a weekly Deep Dive. It is never required, it adds narrative rather than numbers, and your stats are yours either way. If you leave a roster, your record doesn't vanish; it's yours.

What exactly can my coach see?

For athletes they're connected to and have approved: the daily score, every logged meal with its photo and macro read, check‑ins, weight as a trend, requirement completion, and the meal threads. Before you join, the app shows you the real team behind the code and spells out what they'll see — and nothing is shared until you confirm. A parent's dashboard is deliberately narrower: score, grade and date only — and if a family includes a parent in a meal's conversation, they appear there by name, visible to everyone in the thread. Teammates see nothing.

How do I cancel?

Professionals and programs cancel in account settings through Stripe's own portal — no phone call, no retention gauntlet — and can pause instead if that's the better fit. A personal membership cancels in the App Store or Google Play, since that's where the charge lives. In every case the auto‑renewal terms are shown before you're charged, not buried after.

Is this a calorie counter?

No. The athlete never does the counting — the daily habit is one photo per meal; the AI reads the plate and labels every estimate as an estimate; the day is scored on execution — real plates, on time, enough fuel for the work. When there's no plate to photograph, a quick search or a typed nutrition label still logs the meal, but the math stays the app's job, never the athlete's. And an athlete who shouldn't be looking at numbers goes on the Intuitive style, which keeps calorie and macro numbers out of the meal read and scores fueling enough — never restriction.

Can the AI tell me a meal is allergen-free?

No, and it will never claim to. It can flag possible allergens based on the foods it detects and the restrictions you've declared, which is useful as a heads‑up. But a photo can't reveal sauces, oils, cross‑contact, or hidden ingredients, so never rely on it to confirm a plate is safe. People with serious allergies should treat it as one more set of eyes, not a guarantee.

Who can see an athlete's data?

Only the people the athlete (or their family) connects: a coach or trainer sees the day's execution; a parent's dashboard is scoped to the score and trends. Nobody reads a meal thread invisibly — every participant, parent included, is named, and the athlete can open the list of who's in the room. For minors, access requires verified guardian consent and is denied by default until it exists. Data is never sold.
