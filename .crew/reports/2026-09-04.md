## Market scout (Friday)

(For the top of the Daily Ops doc; the Drive connector still can't edit the Doc —

The three most interesting things this week:

1. **Athletes are turning on mandatory calorie tracking, publicly.** College athletes
   told their school paper that Teamworks' meal tracking feeds eating-disorder pressure
   ("wrestling is already prone to eating disorders...") and asked for apps that say
   what TO eat instead of counting what they ate. Every big rival is a calorie counter
   at heart; we're photos, scores, and showing up. That's a position we can claim in
   plain words on the landing page for free — and a design line worth holding: never
   put a raw calorie number in a teen athlete's face by default.

2. **The giant just paid to become us, sort of.** MyFitnessPal bought Cal AI — the
   photo-logging app two teenagers grew to 15M downloads and ~$30M/year — which is the
   market saying photo-first logging won. But Cal AI's own users complain the photo
   estimates wobble on real plates and the billing burns trust. We never pretended a
   photo is a lab measurement; the photo is proof you fueled and the coach does the
   rest. Our honesty rule turns out to be the marketing angle.

3. **Everyone's raising prices, and the team middle market is still empty.**
   MyFitnessPal Premium is up 60% in two years to $79.99/yr, Lose It doubled to match,
   MacroFactor is ~$12/mo with no free tier — while Teamworks only sells enterprise
   deals to athletic departments. Nobody sells a "one coach, one roster, one card"
   plan a high-school or club coach can buy in two minutes. We already have funded
   plans and sponsor flows; the missing piece is a price tag and a story. That one's
   yours to price — details in the backlog (M2).
